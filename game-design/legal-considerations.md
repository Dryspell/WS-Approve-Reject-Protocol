# The Vote Exchange: Legal Considerations

This section covers the legal aspects of The Vote Exchange, including gambling and securities trading considerations. For the core gameplay rules and examples, see [rules.md](./rules.md).

## Gambling Considerations

Gambling is legally considered to be: betting on a game of chance. A game of chance is a game in which the outcome is random or the player does not have significant control over the outcome; it is to be compared to a game of skill. See [Skill Gaming Legal Guide](https://www.firstamendment.com/skill-gaming-legal-guide/) as a starter. Games of skill are nearly always considered not gambling endeavours, instead they are "tournaments with an entry cost and a prize pool" and legally do not have to obey gambling laws (see your own locale for specifics of course). Despite this, for games in which the prize varies by the entries, there is much consternation that the buy-in should be then considered "a wager" and so, obey some set of restrictions. Such laws may be in place depending on the locale. In short, the law may vary very differently locally and applicability of laws will vary wildly depending on monetization methods of the final product.

Despite most people's intuitions, in most states, Poker is legally considered a game of skill with tournaments for a prize pool.

## Securities Trading Considerations

According to Investopedia, "The term 'security' refers to a fungible, negotiable financial instrument that holds some type of monetary value. It represents an ownership position in a publicly-traded corporation via stock; a creditor relationship with a governmental body or a corporation represented by owning that entity's bond; or rights to ownership as represented by an option." see [Investopedia: Security](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/s/security.asp).

This is hard to say, I'm not a lawyer. As a plain reading, votes are indeed expected to be fungible and negotiable financial instruments holding some monetary value. Indeed they have some similar function to an option:

- They are associated with an outside time-frame (the date/time of the vote)
- They carry monetary value based on some independent financial action (vote and distribution of pot vs options as derivatives of underlying stock) and independent free market (there is an options market just like there is The Vote Exchange)
- Like options, a vote may "expire worthless" in the sense that the voter voted in the majority

Our stance throughout development is this thus far:

1. If a vote is legally considered a security, we are not aware of any other specified language that would target its functionality (say restrictions on options trading for options) other than legal language that targets securities in general. In function, we may be creating an entirely novel form of security?
2. We are not certain of the legal stance of any real-money endeavours. We will continue to pursue many avenues to monetization simultaneously as very few in this document are not worth considering.
3. Where we feel issues are legally risky, we will consider our options and evaluate the local legal status in regards to any distribution of the game.
